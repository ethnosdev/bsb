### 2. USFM Specification & Parser Compatibility

#### A. Words of Jesus (`\wj`) placed before verse markers (`\v`)
- **Occurrences**: 163 instances across `MAT.usfm`, `MRK.usfm`, `LUK.usfm`, `JHN.usfm`, `ACT.usfm`, and `REV.usfm`.
- **Examples**:
  - `MAT.usfm:125`: `\p \wj \v 15 “Let it be so now,”\wj* Jesus replied.`
  - `MAT.usfm:143`: `\p \wj \v 10 “Away from Me, Satan!”\wj* Jesus told him.`
  - `MAT.usfm:563`: `\p \wj \v 29 “Come,”\wj* said Jesus.`
  - `MAT.usfm:585`: `\p \wj \v 16 “Do you still not understand?”\wj* Jesus asked. \wj \v 17 “Do you not yet realize...`
  - `MRK.usfm:215`: `...for they were fishermen. \wj \v 19 “Follow Me,” Jesus told them...`
- **Issue**: Under the Paratext USFM specification, `\v` is a milestone/paragraph-level verse marker, while `\wj` is an inline character style. Placing `\wj` *before* `\v` causes standard parsers that segment by `\v` to:
  1. Associate `\wj` with the *preceding* verse (or create an empty row containing only `\wj`).
  2. Strip the opening `\wj` from the verse itself, leaving a closing `\wj*` without a matching opening tag in that verse.
- **Suggested Fix**: Move the inline `\wj` tag after the verse number:
  ```usfm
  \p \v 15 \wj “Let it be so now,”\wj* Jesus replied.
  ```
  and:
  ```usfm
  ...for they were fishermen. \v 19 \wj “Follow Me,” Jesus told them...
  ```

### 3. Marker Semantics & Classification

#### A. Habakkuk 3:19 liturgical postscript marked as `\mr`
- **Location**: `HAB.usfm`, line 179
- **Current text**:
  ```usfm
  \mr For the choirmaster. With stringed instruments.
  ```
- **Issue**: `\mr` is USFM for *Major Section Range* (used for division ranges like "Psalms 1–41"). A liturgical/musical postscript at the conclusion of a psalm is not a section range; it should be marked as `\d` (descriptive title) or `\pc` (centered).

#### B. Psalms Book divisions marked as `\d` instead of `\mr`
- **Location**: `PSA.usfm` at Psalms 1, 42, 73, 90, and 107
- **Current text**:
  ```usfm
  \ms BOOK I
  \d Psalms 1–41
  ```
- **Issue**: `\d` is defined as a *Descriptive Title* (e.g. Psalm superscriptions such as *"A Psalm of David"*). Marking *"Psalms 1–41"* as `\d` causes downstream tools to classify the chapter range as a verse superscription belonging to Psalm 1:1. It should use `\mr` (Major Section Range) beneath the `\ms` header, as in earlier USFM versions:
  ```usfm
  \ms BOOK I
  \mr Psalms 1–41
  ```
